## Incremental route recalibration

The product policy is versioned as `incremental-route-ladder/v1`. The fixed limits of the issue #357 pilot are campaign configuration. Its total budget, per-trial cap, trial slots and time limit do not become permanent routing policy or defaults for later campaigns.

Calibration is scoped by task family, workflow, required capabilities and incumbent seat. A newly available exact model ID challenges the lowest relevant proven seat first. The challenge pins matched fixture versions and seeds, randomizes or counterbalances arm order, prevents context carry between arms, and uses acceptance independent of model identity. A challenger can be promoted only after passing the quality gate and supplying decision evidence for total workflow cost, elapsed time or a required capability. A challenge to the next tier requires a recorded, versioned reason tied to that tier's scope. A tie preserves the incumbent. Exhausting budget, slots or time is inconclusive and cannot promote or demote a route.

Every decision preserves a versioned evidence fingerprint covering the exact model IDs, route scope, policy, fixture set, seeds, arm-order schedule, acceptance rubric, controller, dependencies, tools and capability requirements. A changed dimension invalidates only routes whose fingerprints depend on it. A full matrix rerun is reserved for broad drift: changes to shared policy or acceptance, controller-wide behavior, or evidence that crosses several task families. Historical evidence remains addressable; invalidation changes its applicability rather than rewriting it.

The product-facing `/evaluation/policy` page implements the policy without reading live controller state. It deliberately shows no recommendation until sanitized route evidence is supplied. Its route contract can show the scoped cheapest proven route per task family, accepted and total sample counts, uncertainty interval and method, total workflow cost per accepted result, elapsed time, evidence validity and coverage, and the decision outcome. Total workflow cost is complete only when it includes failed attempts, independent review and repair. Missing uncertainty, cost components, elapsed time or validity is labeled unknown, incomplete, partial or stale instead of being inferred.
