## Complete rule map

The application column gives instructions for Vilya plans. Sections 1–4, 8 and 9
apply to their prose. Section 5 applies to instructions. Section 6 applies to
explanations and notes. Section 7 applies only to actual dangerous or damaging operations.
An absent feature makes its rule inapplicable to that passage, not optional where present.

The source explanations are in Part 1, physical pages 45–128. List introductions
end with a colon. List items have clear markers and initial capitals. Full sentences
end with periods. Fragments have no intermediate periods, commas or semicolons.
The final item ends with a period. Every item must connect grammatically to its introduction.

Parentheses do not hide requirements. Step and paragraph numbering is document
structure and does not count as sentence words. Notes use descriptive limits.
A literal artifact counts according to section 8 when included in prose.
Code, commands, paths, IDs, UI labels, quotations and evidence retain their exact content.
Do not label newly authored prose as code or a quotation to avoid its checks.

## Eight general recommendations

These recommendations supplement the 53 rules. They are not eight additional rules.
Their source explanations are in section 9, physical pages 123–127.

| Recommendation | Vilya application |
| --- | --- |
| GR-1 | Use that to show the start of a dependent clause when it helps the reader. |
| GR-2 | Make the sense of with clear: association, assistance or instrument. Put necessary conditions first. |
| GR-3 | Use approved pronouns with an unmistakable referent. Repeat the noun if necessary. |
| GR-4 | Give the named condition when this can refer to different causes or results. |
| GR-5 | Check the English meaning rather than a similar word in another language. |
| GR-6 | Write English explanations instead of Latin abbreviations where they help the reader. |
| GR-7 | Use neutral language for people and roles. Preserve necessary domain distinctions. |
| GR-8 | Use possessives only when their grammar and relation are clear. |

## Dictionary consultation and technical terms

Consult the dictionary introduction, physical pages 131–148, before unfamiliar lookups.
For a revised passage, examine the necessary entries in physical pages 149–434.
Read the permitted meaning, grammatical function, forms, examples and all related help.
An entry's presence alone does not approve the intended use.
A lowercase entry gives alternatives, not unrestricted permission.

Prefer accurate approved wording. If an alternative changes the meaning, reconstruct
the sentence. If necessary, consult an English dictionary to identify the intended
sense before selecting an STE alternative. Dictionary help can restrict context,
forms or meanings. Reuse sufficient prior lookup evidence only for unchanged senses and forms.

This small glossary gives necessary Vilya meanings, not a blanket exception for jargon.
The categories refer to rule 1.5 for nouns and rule 1.12 for verbs.
Terms are established by the repository's issue, Git and host contracts.
Other terms require their own precise meaning, function and category before use.

The same spelling can require different justification in different positions.
A technical noun does not authorize a verb. A technical verb does not authorize a noun.
An adjectival past participle of a justified technical verb can modify its technical noun.
For example, rebase describes a Git operation. It does not describe rewriting an ordinary sentence.

## Existing plan review

Use relevant sections on demand. Reuse verified interpretations and term evidence
only while their wording, meaning and context remain applicable. Recheck changes.
The complete map establishes coverage, not a compulsory full-map reread at every entry.
Detailed matrices below are #373 delivery evidence, not recurring plan attachments.
Do not emit per-plan rule checklists, per-word tables or glossary dumps.

Do these checks within the existing plan review:

1. Identify the procedural passages, descriptive passages and protected artifacts.
2. Consult the applicable explanations where verified interpretations are missing or changed.
3. Account for applicability, including passage-specific reasons for absent features.
4. Do the sentence, paragraph, list, grammar and vocabulary checks.
5. Retain evidence for consulted entry senses, functions, forms and help.
6. Give necessary technical terms their meanings, functions and categories.
7. Compare the source task and the new text for semantic preservation.
8. Give a short actual-check receipt with reusable evidence links and actionable exceptions.
9. If necessary checks are incomplete, stop before a readiness or conformance claim.

Compare actor, action, condition, order, quantity, obligation, permission and uncertainty.
Also compare ownership, exclusions, evidence, alternatives, costs, acceptance, repair history
and stops. Do not replace advice with an obligation or possibility with permission.
The may and should alternatives require a meaning check. MUST expresses obligation.
CAN permits possibility, ability or permission, so context must identify the intended sense.
RECOMMEND can retain advice without changing it into a command.
